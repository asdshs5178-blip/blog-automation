Korea Winter Weather and Packing Guide: Seoul Temperatures, Cold Wave Alerts, Fine Dust (2026)

Short answer: Korean winters are cold and dry. In Seoul, the official 30-year normals put the January mean at -1.9°C, December mornings average -3.4°C, and cold snaps can push mornings below -12°C, which is the threshold for a cold wave advisory. Busan and Jeju are noticeably milder. You will want a windproof coat, layers you can take off indoors, shoes with grip, and a plan for two things that surprise many visitors: cold wave alerts and fine dust days. This post covers the numbers from the Korea Meteorological Administration (KMA), what the alerts mean, and what to pack for December to February.

How cold does Korea get in winter?

The figures in this post are KMA climate normals for 1991-2020. KMA recalculates these 30-year averages every ten years, following World Meteorological Organization practice, so they are the current official baseline. Normals are long-term averages, so a given week can be much warmer or colder. The Fahrenheit numbers are my own rounded conversions.

Gyeongbokgung Palace in Seoul after a winter snowfall. Photo: Korea.net / Korean Culture and Information Service (Jeon Han, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons.

Seoul's coldest month is January, with a monthly mean of -1.9°C (29°F). December is a little milder on paper, but it is the month most visitors arrive in, and the table below shows what a typical December day looks like in Seoul and six other cities.

Two things the table does not show. First, the cold comes in waves: a few days of -10°C mornings, then a few days near freezing. Second, the air is dry and the wind in Seoul can make the same temperature feel much colder than it does in a humid climate. Lip balm and moisturizer are not a luxury here.

Temperatures outside Seoul

Korea is small, but winter temperatures vary a lot from north to south and from inland to coast. Busan's average December low (2.0°C) is above freezing and Jeju's (5.6°C) is closer to a cool autumn night in Seoul. If you are splitting a trip between Seoul and the south coast, pack for Seoul and you will be fine everywhere. Inland and northern Gyeonggi towns are colder than Seoul: the KMA normals give January means of -3.8°C for Dongducheon and -4.6°C for Paju, both within an hour of the capital.

What a cold wave alert means

From October to April, KMA issues two levels of cold wave alert. You may see them on the KMA website, in news apps, or as a buzzing emergency text on your phone.

  • Cold wave advisory (한파주의보): issued when the morning low is expected to stay at or below -12°C for two or more days, or when the morning low is expected to fall 10°C or more from the previous day to 3°C or below and at least 3°C below normal, or when sudden cold is expected to cause serious damage.
  • Cold wave warning (한파경보): the stronger level, issued when the morning low is expected to stay at or below -15°C for two or more days, or when the drop is 15°C or more under the same conditions as above.

For a traveler, an advisory mostly means: cover your ears and hands, shorten long outdoor walks, and expect icy pavements the next morning. Palaces and outdoor markets stay open on ordinary cold days. KMA publishes an English forecast site with current severe weather status and daily highs and lows at weather.go.kr/neng. On its short-range page, the morning low is the lowest reading between 3 AM and 9 AM, which is the number that matters for the alert.

What to pack for Korea in winter

Indoor heating is strong. Homes, cafes and many guesthouses use ondol, heated floors, and subway cars have heated seats. Layering matters more than one very heavy coat.

  • A windproof, knee-length or longer coat. Down padded coats are what most locals wear in January, though plenty of people manage with a wool coat plus layers.
  • Thermal base layer or a sweater you can remove indoors.
  • Hat, gloves and a scarf or neck warmer. Touchscreen gloves help with maps and transit apps.
  • Shoes with grip and some insulation. Snow is not constant in Seoul, but melted snow refreezes overnight on side streets and stairs.
  • Lip balm, hand cream and moisturizer for dry air.
  • Disposable hot packs (핫팩, hot-pack). Convenience stores sell them in winter; shake and keep in a pocket. Do not place them directly on skin for long.
  • A KF94 mask or similar for fine dust days (see below). Pharmacies and convenience stores sell them.
  • A reusable water bottle. Heated indoor air is drying.

What you can skip: heavy snow boots for a Seoul city trip, unless you plan ski resorts or Gangwon mountains. Umbrellas are rarely needed; Seoul averages only about 23 mm of precipitation in December.

Reading fine dust (미세먼지) forecasts

Winter and early spring bring the worst fine dust days, when cold, still air traps pollution. Korea grades daily air quality in four steps, and the PM2.5 grade is the one most apps show first.

GradePM2.5 daily average (µg/m³)
Good (좋음)0-15
Moderate (보통)16-35
Bad (나쁨)36-75
Very bad (매우나쁨)76 and above

For the coarser PM10, the "Bad" grade starts at 81 µg/m³. Separately, city and provincial governments issue a PM2.5 advisory when the hourly average stays at 75 µg/m³ or higher for two hours, and a warning at 150 µg/m³ or higher for two hours. On advisory days the official guidance is for children, older people and anyone with lung or heart conditions to limit time outdoors, and for everyone to cut back on long or strenuous outdoor activity. Real-time readings by district are on the national Air Korea site at airkorea.or.kr, and most Korean weather apps show the dust grade next to the temperature.

Getting emergency alerts in English

Korea sends emergency texts (재난문자) in Korean for cold waves, heavy snow, fine dust warnings and other events. For a translated version, the Ministry of the Interior and Safety offers the free Emergency Ready App, the foreign-language edition of its domestic safety app, on the App Store and Play Store. It delivers translated alerts, action guides, shelter and emergency room locations, and embassy contacts. Since 15 June 2026 it supports 22 languages, including English, up from three before. After installing, open Settings and choose the alert types and the region you want to receive; on Android you pick the language inside the app, while on iPhone the app follows your device language.

For non-emergency help in English, the Korea Tourism Organization's 1330 travel hotline operates 24 hours a day, 365 days a year, offers interpretation, and charges only the normal call fee. Dial 1330 in Korea or +82-2-1330 from abroad.

Winter travel logistics

  1. Flights and airport buses. Heavy snow can delay flights and slow the expressway from Incheon. On a snowy morning the AREX train is the more predictable option; see Incheon Airport to Seoul: AREX, bus or taxi.
  2. Subway over walking on icy days. Stations are heated, cars have heated seats, and you avoid frozen pavements. A transit card keeps you from fumbling with cash in gloves; the options are compared in T-money, Climate Card or WOWPASS.
  3. Check the next morning's low before an early outing. Sunrise spots, hikes and palace openings are the coldest part of the day.
  4. Hot drinks and heated spaces are everywhere. Convenience stores sell hot canned coffee and tea from warmers near the counter, and a jjimjilbang (Korean bathhouse) is a reasonable way to spend a -10°C evening.

Common mistakes

  • Packing one heavy coat and no layers, then overheating on the subway and in restaurants.
  • Judging the day by the afternoon high. A December day in Seoul with a 4°C high usually started near -3°C, and January mornings run colder.
  • Ignoring the dust grade. Blue sky does not mean clean air, and a hazy day is not necessarily a dust day. Check the number.
  • Thinking Busan is warm. Milder than Seoul, yes, but December still averages under 6°C and the sea wind is sharp.
  • Assuming the Korean emergency text is about you. Many are routine notices. Translate it or check the Emergency Ready App before worrying.
