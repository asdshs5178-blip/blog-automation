Gyeongbokgung Palace Hours and Tickets: 3,000 Won, Closed Tuesdays, Hanbok Rules

Short answer: Gyeongbokgung Palace in Seoul costs 3,000 won for adults, is closed every Tuesday, and runs on seasonal hours. From November to February it is open 09:00–17:00 with last entry at 16:00; in March–May and September–October it is open until 18:00; in June–August until 18:30. Entry is free if you wear a hanbok that meets the official guideline (a top and a bottom, not just one piece), for children aged 6 and under, and for visitors aged 65 and over. The royal guard changing ceremony runs at 10:00 and 14:00, and free English guided tours leave at 11:00, 13:30 and 15:30 on days the palace is open. The details below come from the Korea Heritage Service's Royal Palaces and Tombs Center pages that run the palace.

Opening hours by season

The palace changes its closing time three times a year. Last entry is one hour before closing. In January–February and November–December the hours are 09:00–17:00 (last entry 16:00); in March–May and September–October 09:00–18:00 (last entry 17:00); and in June–August 09:00–18:30 (last entry 17:30).

The winter schedule catches a lot of people. If you arrive at 16:10 in December, you will not get in. Plan the palace for the morning or early afternoon, and dress for the cold, because almost everything is outdoors. My earlier post on Korea in winter covers what to pack.

Closed days: every Tuesday, with one exception

Gyeongbokgung, Jongmyo Shrine and Chilgung close on Tuesdays. Changdeokgung, Deoksugung, Changgyeonggung, the Joseon royal tombs and Sungnyemun close on Mondays instead. So if your only free day is a Tuesday, Changdeokgung or Deoksugung are open; if it is a Monday, Gyeongbokgung is.

There is a holiday rule to know. When the Tuesday closing day falls on a public holiday (including substitute holidays), the palace opens that day, and the first non-holiday after the holiday becomes the closing day instead. The guard ceremony and guided tours also skip Tuesdays, so a Tuesday visit to the outer square only gets you the gate, not the show.

Tickets: 3,000 won and who gets in free

Day tickets are sold at the booths on site, and you do not need to reserve for a normal daytime visit. Adult admission is 3,000 won (2,400 won per person for paying groups of 10 or more), and for foreign visitors the adult rate applies from age 19 to 64 (for Koreans, 25 to 64). The official fee pages list the free categories for foreign visitors as children aged 6 and under, seniors aged 65 and over, and anyone wearing hanbok; bring ID or a passport so staff can check age at the entrance.

The integrated ticket is worth it if you plan on two or more palaces. It costs 6,000 won, is valid for six months from purchase, is sold at the ticket booths of the four palaces and Jongmyo, and does not include the Changdeokgung Secret Garden (Huwon). Some older pages and blogs still quote 10,000 won and three months; the current Royal Palaces and Tombs Center fee page shows 6,000 won. For the Secret Garden, you either reserve online (from 10:00 six days before your visit until the day before) or buy a same-day ticket from 09:00 at Changdeokgung.

Holiday periods are a separate case. In recent years the palaces have opened free during the Seollal and Chuseok holidays; for 2026 Chuseok, Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung, Deoksugung, Changgyeonggung, Jongmyo and the royal tombs were free from 24 to 27 September, with the Changdeokgung Secret Garden excluded. During the 2024 Seollal free opening, the palace's notice stated that no regular guided tours would run. Whether the same applies for Seollal 2027 will be announced closer to the date. The 2027 dates are in my Korea public holidays 2027 post.

Hanbok free entry: what counts and what does not

The free-entry rule is real, but it is checked at the gate, and the guideline is more specific than "wear something traditional." The Korea Heritage Service publishes a hanbok guideline. The main points:

  • Both traditional hanbok and modern everyday-style hanbok (saenghwal hanbok) are accepted.
  • You need a hanbok top (jeogori) and a hanbok bottom (skirt or trousers). A jeogori worn with jeans, or a hanbok skirt or trousers worn with a T-shirt, does not qualify.
  • A T-shirt-style top sold as a hanbok jacket is not counted as a jeogori. A jacket with an overlapping collar counts even if it has no ribbon ties. A one-piece dress without a jeogori does not count.
  • A durumagi overcoat worn on its own is not enough; the top and bottom must be there underneath.
  • The rule applies regardless of nationality; foreign visitors in hanbok are included.

Rental shops cluster around the palace and Bukchon. When you rent, ask for a full set (top and bottom) and say you want it for free palace entry; shops near the palace deal with this question constantly. I am not recommending any particular shop here, and prices vary, so compare a couple.

Guard changing ceremony: 10:00 and 14:00

The Sumunjang (royal gate guard) changing ceremony is the most photographed event at the palace. It takes place in the Heungnyemun Gate area twice a day, at 10:00 and 14:00, lasts about 20 minutes, and involves roughly 70 guards and a 15-member band; it runs all year except Tuesdays and other closing days. The ceremony happens in the courtyard between Gwanghwamun and Heungnyemun, before the ticket check, so you can watch it without buying a ticket. A shorter gate-guard inspection ritual is held at Gwanghwamun at 11:00 and 13:00.

Weather can cancel it. In August 2026 the ceremony was suspended from 5 August under a heat wave emergency alert in Seoul and resumed on 8 August once the alert was lifted, and the Royal Palaces and Tombs Center said it would run the ceremony flexibly depending on weather conditions. The official event page also notes that operations may be suspended depending on the weather, so check the notices page on a bad-weather day.

Free English guided tours

There is no need to book a paid tour to get an explanation. English tours led by the palace's own guides run at 11:00, 13:30 and 15:30 every day except Tuesday, and take about 70 minutes. Note that the English tour does not start at 10:00 or 14:00; those are the guard ceremony times. A good sequence is: watch the 10:00 ceremony, buy your ticket, then join the 11:00 tour. The official page warns that times and frequency can change, so glance at the schedule board inside the gate when you arrive.

Getting there, how long to allow, and night openings

Take Seoul Subway Line 3 to Gyeongbokgung Station; exit 5 leads straight to the palace grounds. Gwanghwamun Station on Line 5 is also a short walk to the main gate across Gwanghwamun Square. My transit card guide covers how to pay for the subway.

Allow 90 minutes for the central axis from Gwanghwamun to the royal residences and Gyeonghoeru Pavilion; allow half a day if you add the ceremony, an English tour and one of the two free museums on the grounds (the National Palace Museum of Korea and the National Folk Museum of Korea, which keep their own hours).

Night openings run twice a year, in spring and autumn, and tickets are separate. For the autumn 2026 run, the Royal Palaces and Tombs Center notice gave dates of 2 September to 2 October, no night viewing on Mondays and Tuesdays, hours 19:00–21:30 with last admission at 20:30, and a price of 3,000 won. Tickets were sold online through NOL Ticket at 3,000 per day (up to four per person, bookable until the day before), with 300 on-site tickets per day for foreigners only (up to two per person, passport required) at the Gwanghwamun ticket office; free-entry visitors, including those in hanbok, showed ID at Heungnyemun without booking. The 2027 dates have not been announced as of this writing; they usually appear on the notices page a few weeks ahead and sell out fast.

Common mistakes

  1. Arriving on a Tuesday. Swap in Changdeokgung or Deoksugung, which are open that day.
  2. Arriving after 16:00 between November and February. The gate is shut for entry even though the palace closes at 17:00.
  3. Renting only a hanbok jacket or a one-piece dress and expecting free entry. Staff check for a top and a bottom.
  4. Assuming the 10:00 ceremony is followed by a 10:00 English tour. The first English tour is at 11:00.
  5. Expecting the regular guided tours during Seollal or Chuseok free-opening periods. Past holiday notices have listed no regular tours on those days.
  6. Paying 10,000 won for an "integrated ticket" quoted on an old page. Check the current price at the booth; the official page lists 6,000 won.
