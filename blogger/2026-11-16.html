Korea Public Holidays 2027: Full List, Seollal and Chuseok Dates, and What Closes

Short answer: South Korea has 24 public holidays on top of Sundays in 2027, 7 of them substitute holidays, which makes 10 breaks of three days or more for anyone on a five-day week. The two that matter most for visitors are Seollal (Lunar New Year), a four-day break from 6 to 9 February 2027, and Chuseok, 14 to 16 September 2027. Before that, Christmas 2026 and New Year's Day 2027 both fall on a Friday. On public holidays, banks, government offices and immigration offices close, but subways, buses, convenience stores, palaces and most big attractions stay open. The main exceptions are the actual days of Seollal and Chuseok, when many small shops and family-run restaurants shut and trains sell out weeks ahead.

The dates below come from the 2027 calendar standard (월력요항) published by the Korea AeroSpace Administration on 29 June 2026, which is the official basis for every printed calendar in the country. Coming up first: Christmas 2026 and New Year 2027

If you are visiting over the coming winter, two holidays sit inside the trip window. Christmas Day, 25 December 2026, is a Friday, and New Year's Day, 1 January 2027, is also a Friday, so each gives a three-day weekend. Neither is a family-gathering holiday in the way Seollal and Chuseok are. Cafes, department stores and restaurants in tourist areas are generally busy rather than closed, though a few independent places take the day off. Banks, post offices and public offices close on both days. The National Museum of Korea is one notable closure on 1 January (details below). Seollal and Chuseok: the two holidays that change your trip

Seollal and Chuseok are the only holidays where a large share of the population travels at the same time. People head to their hometowns, so Seoul itself can feel unusually quiet, while trains, intercity buses and expressways out of the capital are packed on the first day and packed coming back on the last day.

  • Seollal 2027: the longest break of the year, running from 6 to 9 February including the substitute holiday, four days in total. Seollal day itself is Sunday 7 February.
  • Chuseok 2027: Chuseok day is Wednesday 15 September, and the official holiday runs from Tuesday 14 to Thursday 16 September. Expect many offices to bridge the Monday and Friday with leave.

What this means in practice: on the holiday day itself, many small restaurants, markets and family-run shops close for one to three days. Chain restaurants, convenience stores, hotel restaurants and shops in Myeongdong, Hongdae or large malls mostly stay open. Traditional markets such as Namdaemun or Gwangjang often reduce hours. If a specific restaurant matters to you, check its Naver Map listing or Instagram a few days before, because holiday closures are usually posted there rather than on a website.

How substitute holidays work

When a public holiday lands on a Saturday or Sunday, the next weekday that is not already a holiday becomes a day off. In 2027 this happens seven times. The substitute days apply to Seollal (9 Feb), Labor Day (3 May), Constitution Day (19 Jul), Liberation Day (16 Aug), National Foundation Day (4 Oct), Hangeul Day (11 Oct) and Christmas (27 Dec). Memorial Day on 6 June is the one holiday in the list that does not get a replacement, so that Sunday is simply a Sunday. Labor Day and Constitution Day became public holidays, with substitute days, in 2026, so 2027 is the first calendar standard to include them from the start; older calendars and English-language lists may not show them.

Palaces and museums on holidays

Seoul's palaces are a safe plan for a holiday. Gyeongbokgung normally closes on Tuesdays, but when the closing day coincides with a public holiday, including substitute holidays, the palace opens, and the first non-holiday day after that becomes the closing day instead. Changdeokgung, Changgyeonggung and Deoksugung close on Mondays under the same rule. So on Monday 4 October 2027 or Monday 27 December 2027, the Monday-closing palaces will be open and will rest on the Tuesday. For Seollal and Chuseok, the Royal Palaces and Tombs Center has gone further in recent years: over Seollal 2026 the four palaces, Jongmyo and the royal tombs opened free of charge from 14 to 18 February with no closing day (the Changdeokgung Secret Garden excepted), and then all closed on 19 February, the day after the break. Expect a similar announcement a few weeks before each holiday. Opening hours are shorter in winter: from January to February and November to December, Gyeongbokgung is open 09:00 to 17:00 with last entry at 16:00.

Museums are the exception to check. The National Museum of Korea in Yongsan, which is free and normally open seven days a week, closes on 1 January, Seollal day and Chuseok day; in 2026 those were 17 February and 25 September. Expect the same pattern on 1 January, 7 February and 15 September 2027, and confirm on the museum's own page before you go, since the permanent galleries also close on the first Monday of March, June, September and December for maintenance.

Getting train tickets for Seollal and Chuseok

Korail does not sell Seollal and Chuseok tickets the normal way. It runs a separate advance sale about a month before, and the 2027 schedule has not been announced yet. For reference, here is how the 2026 Seollal sale worked:

  1. Korail opened its 2026 Seollal advance sale on 15 January, covering trains running for six days from 13 to 18 February.
  2. There were no counter sales. Reservations went through the Korail Talk app and a dedicated holiday booking page on the Korail website, for registered railway members only, with a phone line (1544-8545) for people who cannot use the internet.
  3. The first two days, 15 and 16 January (09:00–15:00), were reserved for seniors, registered disabled persons and national merit holders. The general sale for everyone ran from 19 to 21 January, 07:00 to 13:00, with different train lines on different days and the Gyeongbu line (Seoul–Busan) on the last day. Leftover seats went on normal sale from the afternoon of 21 January.
  4. Payment opened at midnight on 22 January; general-sale tickets had to be paid by 25 January and priority tickets by 28 January. Unpaid tickets were cancelled automatically and passed to people on the waiting list.

Cancellations trickle back in the days before the holiday, so it is worth checking the app repeatedly. Express buses are the usual fallback and tend to have space longer. SRT, the separate high-speed operator from Suseo Station, ran its own sale on different dates (26 to 29 January in 2026) for the same travel period. If your dates are flexible, the simplest move is to avoid travelling out of Seoul on the first day of the break and back on the last day.

Common mistakes

  • Planning a bank visit or immigration appointment on a holiday. Offices are closed, and the days right after Seollal and Chuseok are crowded. Residents should book Hi Korea appointments well around the break.
  • Assuming Christmas works like Seollal. It does not. Christmas is a one-day holiday with normal city life; Seollal is the day when the family restaurant you wanted will be shut.
  • Trusting an old holiday list. Labor Day and Constitution Day only became official public holidays in 2026, and substitute holidays have expanded over the years. Use the current year's list.
  • Showing up at a palace on its closing day the day after a holiday. The rest day moves, so Gyeongbokgung may be closed on a Wednesday after a Tuesday holiday, and all palaces closed together the day after the Seollal break in 2026.
  • Leaving KTX tickets to the last week. For regular long weekends this is usually fine; for Seollal and Chuseok it often is not.
