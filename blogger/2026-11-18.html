Seoul to Busan by KTX: 54,400 Won, About 2.5 Hours, and How to Book (2026)

Short answer: A standard-class KTX ticket from Seoul Station to Busan Station costs 54,400 won one way since 1 September 2026, when Korail took over the SRT service and lowered KTX fares by an average of 10 percent. Most trains take roughly two and a half hours. You can book up to two months ahead on the Korail+ app or the English Korail website, and trains also leave from Suseo Station on the Gangnam side of Seoul. For a single round trip, regular tickets are cheaper than the KORAIL Pass.

What changed in September 2026: KTX and SRT became one

If you read older guides, you will see two separate high-speed operators: KTX from Seoul Station and SRT from Suseo Station, each with its own app and fare table. That is no longer the case. On 1 September 2026, after the Fair Trade Commission approved the merger in early August and the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport completed the remaining approvals, Korail and SR were combined into a single operator and the Suseo trains were folded into the KTX network.

A KTX high-speed train waiting at a platform in Seoul Station. Photo: revi, CC BY 2.0 kr, via Wikimedia Commons.

The practical effects for a visitor are these, based on Korail's August 2026 announcement:

  • KTX fares were lowered by an average of 10 percent to match the old SRT fare level. Seoul–Busan went from 59,800 won to 54,400 won.
  • One app, Korail+ (코레일+), now sells tickets for both Seoul Station and Suseo Station departures in one search. It was released on 3 August 2026 as an update to the old KorailTalk app, so if you already had KorailTalk, updating it gives you Korail+. The old SRT app was kept running for a while but only for non-member bookings without the merged benefits, so use Korail+.
  • Trains from Suseo now earn 5 percent Korail mileage and qualify for the same 30 percent transfer discount onto conventional trains as other KTX trains.
  • Korail added seats, about 16,000 per day or 116,000 per week across the network, so weekend availability should be a little better than before.

The lowered fare is part of a three-year business plan that Korail submitted to the Fair Trade Commission and the ministry. If that holds, the 54,400 won figure could change again around September 2029, so treat it as a current fare, not a permanent one.

Seoul Station or Suseo Station: which one to use

Both stations have direct high-speed trains to Busan. The right one depends on where you are staying.

  • Seoul Station is the main terminal, north of the Han River. It is on subway Line 1, Line 4 and the AREX airport railroad, so it is the easy choice if you are coming straight from Incheon Airport or staying around Myeongdong, Jongno or Hongdae. Our guide to getting from Incheon Airport to Seoul covers the AREX connection.
  • Suseo Station is in the southeast of Seoul, on subway Line 3, the Suin-Bundang Line and GTX-A. It is convenient if you are staying in Gangnam, Jamsil or Songpa and want to avoid crossing the city.

A ticket is valid only from the station printed on it, so double-check the departure station before you tap "pay." Seoul Station and Suseo Station are on opposite sides of the city, far enough apart by subway to miss a train.

How to book, step by step

  1. Check the booking window. Since 1 October 2026, Korail sells regular tickets from two months before the departure date, up from one month previously; sales open at 7 a.m. Korean time on that day. Seollal and Chuseok tickets follow a separate special-reservation schedule that Korail announces each year; see our 2027 public holiday list for the dates to plan around.
  2. Pick a channel. Download Korail+ (search for "Korail" in your app store; the app has an English setting) or use the English Korail site at letskorail.com. You can also buy at station ticket counters and ticket machines on the day, which works fine outside holidays and Friday evenings.
  3. Search Seoul to Busan (or Suseo to Busan) and compare arrival times. Filter for "KTX" if other train types show up; Mugunghwa and ITX trains are much slower on this route.
  4. Choose a seat. Forward-facing seats and window seats go first. If you are travelling with luggage, a seat near the car ends is closer to the luggage racks.
  5. Pay by card. Some travellers report that foreign cards are not accepted on Korail's own channels at times. If your payment fails, a station counter is a reliable fallback, and you can also buy a ticket on arrival at Seoul Station for a later train the same day.
  6. Keep the ticket live in the app or on paper. Staff scan the ticket from the app itself, not from a screenshot. If you booked online, you do not need to collect a paper ticket. Under the merged rules, a ticket can be changed without a fee up to 30 minutes before departure (to a train within seven days either way), and a refund within 10 minutes of payment carries no penalty.

Is the KORAIL Pass worth it for this trip?

The KORAIL Pass is a flat-rate rail pass sold only to foreign visitors. In its current form it is a "select" pass: you choose 2, 3, 4 or 5 travel days to use within 10 days of the start date, with up to two reserved seats per day. It covers KTX and other Korail trains but not city subways.

Now the arithmetic. A Seoul–Busan round trip in standard class is 108,800 won. The cheapest pass is 131,000 won. So if Busan is your only train trip, buying two regular tickets saves you 22,200 won (my calculation). The pass starts to pay off when you ride two long legs on the same day, for example a Seoul–Busan day trip, or when you string together Busan, Gyeongju and another city within a few days. Price your actual legs in the app first, then decide.

These pass prices match what several recent guides report from Korail's site in October and early November 2026, but I could not load the official pass page directly, and at least one older page still lists a different price structure, so confirm the number on letskorail.com before you buy.

Getting to the station and riding the train

  • Arrive 15 to 20 minutes before departure. There is no security check or passport control; you walk to the platform and board. Platform numbers appear on the departure boards shortly before the train leaves.
  • Standard-class seats have a fold-down tray and a shared armrest. Power outlets are available on most trains but not at every seat, so charge your phone beforehand.
  • Large suitcases go on the racks at the ends of each car. There is no checked luggage.
  • Busan Station is on Busan Metro Line 1. From there, Nampo and Jagalchi are a few stops south, and Seomyeon is a few stops north. A T-money card from Seoul works on Busan buses and the metro; see our transit card comparison.
  • Taxis queue outside the main exit of Busan Station and use meters.

Common mistakes

  • Buying from the wrong Seoul station. Check whether your ticket says Seoul or Suseo before you leave your hotel.
  • Choosing the earliest departure instead of the earliest arrival. A train leaving 20 minutes later can arrive first.
  • Relying on old SRT information. SR no longer exists as a separate operator, SRT-only fares are gone, and the old SRT app gives you at most a non-member booking with none of the merged benefits. Book everything through Korail+ or the Korail website.
  • Leaving Friday evening or Sunday afternoon bookings to the last minute. These are the busiest slots for Seoul–Busan, and standing tickets are not a comfortable fallback for a 2.5-hour ride.
  • Assuming the pass is a bargain. For one round trip it is not. Do the math above.
